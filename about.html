About Up NEXT

We teach kids to build — not just consume.

Up NEXT is a kids coding school in Kampala. We help children aged 5–13+ move from playing with technology to creating with it — one project at a time.

Our story

Why we started

Most children today grow up surrounded by technology — but very few ever get to understand it. They play games, watch videos and tap screens, yet the moment something breaks or they want to change it, they're stuck.

Up NEXT exists to close that gap. We started with a simple belief: every child can learn to build. Not because they'll all become programmers, but because learning to break a problem into steps, test an idea and fix what went wrong is a skill that matters in every part of life.

Since then we've taught children as young as five to make their first animation, and teenagers to publish their first website. Every one of them leaves with something they made themselves.

How we teach

Our teaching philosophy

Six principles shape every lesson we run, from a six-year-old's first ScratchJr story to a teenager's capstone project.

01

Start with something they want to make

A game about their dog, a website about football, an animation about their family. Interest drives effort — so we always begin with what a child cares about, then teach the coding needed to build it.

02

Model it, then do it together, then let go

Every new concept follows the same rhythm: the teacher demonstrates, the class builds it as a group, then students try it independently. Support fades as confidence grows.

03

Keep screens to half the lesson

Especially for younger children, we mix tablet time with unplugged games, paper mazes and movement. Learning to think like a programmer doesn't always need a computer.

04

Teach debugging as a habit, not a punishment

Something not working is the most useful moment in the room. We teach a simple ritual — test, find, fix, retest — and praise children who spot their own mistakes.

05

Make progress visible

Badges, portfolios, progress reports and end-of-term showcases. Children need to see how far they've come, and parents deserve to know what they're paying for.

06

Finish with a showcase

Every course and camp ends with children presenting what they built to parents and peers. Presenting is a skill in itself — and it turns a hobby into an achievement.

Safety

Safeguarding and safety

Children learn best when parents feel completely at ease. Here's how we keep our classrooms safe.

✓
All teachers background-checked and reference-verified
✓
Never fewer than two adults present during any class or camp
✓
Supervised drop-off and pick-up — children only released to named adults
✓
First-aid trained staff on site during every session
✓
Filtered internet and monitored devices — no open browsing
✓
Photos only taken and shared with written parental consent
✓
Allergies and dietary needs recorded before camp begins
✓
Clear behaviour policy shared with parents at enrolment
✓
Open-door policy — parents welcome to visit any session
